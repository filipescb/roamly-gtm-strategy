# Competitive Intelligence & Strategic Bet — Roamly Groups

> Module 2 · Competitive Intelligence — ★ Deliverable 2
>
> A battlecard that maps the competitive landscape and the one strategic bet you'll make to win.

**Target segment (from Module 1):** The trip organizer within friend groups planning celebration trips (bachelor/bachelorette parties, reunions, milestone birthdays) — full buying authority, but acute pain from chasing friends for payments, juggling fragmented bookings, and manually tracking who's confirmed. Refined post-battlecard: the segment that actually wins is the organizer whose trip has outgrown what a group chat and Venmo can hold together — i.e., a multi-day, multi-activity trip for a larger group (6+ people) — not every organizer planning a celebration trip.

## 1. The competitive set

_Who really competes for this job — including the "do nothing" / spreadsheet alternative?_

| Competitor | What they do well | Where they're weak | How we win |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Airbnb** (Homes + relaunched Experiences) | Native, free, already-proven Split Payments feature for group trips; zero switching cost since the friend group already has accounts, trust, and payment methods on file; massive brand gravity for "planning a trip with friends" | Experiences program has been paused/relaunched multiple times since 2024 — publicly documented inconsistency and host frustration ("Experiences whiplash"); split payments were built for splitting a *lodging* bill, not for coordinating a shared *itinerary* of activities | Win on curation and vetting depth (our "vetted local expert" model vs. their still-recovering Experiences supply), and on purpose-built coordination — one flow spanning booking + payment + itinerary for the *activity*, not just the stay |
| **GetYourGuide** | Massive selection (10M+ experiences booked/quarter), price competition, large-scale trust infrastructure (reviews, ratings); strong for fast, low-effort activity discovery | Breadth over curation — largely generic, mass-market tours, the opposite of "vetted local expert" positioning; no group-payment-splitting or shared-itinerary coordination for a private friend group at all | Win on curation quality and on solving the coordination problem entirely — GYG gets you an activity booked, but the group still has to coordinate manually afterward |
| **WeTravel** | Purpose-built split payments + itinerary tooling — the closest functional match to the exact organizer pain we're targeting | Built for tour operators/retreat leaders running trips as a business, not a friend organizing one weekend — disproportionate setup overhead for a one-off trip; zero built-in supply of vetted local experiences | Win on being purpose-built for *this* buyer (a friend, not a business) and by pairing coordination tooling with real curated supply, which WeTravel doesn't have |
| **Status quo: DIY (group chat + spreadsheet + Venmo)** | Zero adoption friction — no download, no account, everyone already has these apps open; totally flexible; free; no CAC required to "acquire" a user into it since it's already running by default | All coordination labor sits on one person; no visibility for the group into what's booked or owed; no payment enforcement; itinerary and payments never sync with actual bookings; breaks down as group size/complexity increases | Win once coordination pain exceeds a threshold — the more complex the trip, the more the DIY stack collapses while our single connected flow doesn't; **we do not try to win the simple, low-complexity trips where DIY wins on pure effort** |

## 2. Battlecard

- **Our one-line advantage:** One connected flow — booking, split payment, and shared itinerary in a single system — versus a stitched-together stack of disconnected tools (free or paid) that never sync with each other.
- **Trap to set / question to plant:** _"What happens the next time someone in the group 'forgets' to Venmo you back — and how would you even know, three weeks later, who still owes what?"_
- **Objection → response:**
  - _"It's free, this isn't."_ → Reframe cost as time and social friction, not money — the organizer is already paying with hours of chasing and awkward reminders.
  - _"My friends won't sign up for a new app."_ → Guests interact as guests — pay and view the itinerary without creating an account; only the organizer needs to be a real user.
  - _"We've always done it this way."_ → Don't argue against habit — ask what happened last time someone paid late or the itinerary fell out of sync. Let their own experience make the case.

## 3. The strategic bet

_The single, defensible bet for Roamly Groups. Not a feature list — a wager about the market._

> We are betting that **Roamly Groups wins by becoming the system of record for complex group trips — the one place where the booking, the payment, and the plan stay in sync automatically**, because **split payments as a mechanic is already commoditized (Airbnb has offered it free since 2018) — the real, unowned gap in the market is the connective tissue between the payment, the itinerary, and the activity, which no competitor has purpose-built for a friend-group organizer.**

- **Why now:** Airbnb's Experiences program is publicly unsettled (paused and relaunched multiple times since 2024), creating a real trust and quality gap in curated group activities right when Roamly's host network has scaled to 12,000+ vetted experts across four continents — the supply-side credibility to win this moment exists now in a way it may not have a year or two ago.
- **What we're deliberately NOT doing:** Deprioritizing simple, low-complexity celebration trips (small groups, one or two bookings) as a v1 target — even though they technically fall inside the original "celebration trip organizer" segment — because that's exactly where the free DIY stack wins on effort alone, and no amount of positioning or product polish changes that math.
- **What would prove the bet right within 90 days:** A measurable guest-to-signup conversion rate (non-account trip participants creating a Roamly account within 90 days of a group trip) concentrated disproportionately in larger, multi-activity trips rather than spread evenly across all trip sizes — evidence that the complexity threshold, not the occasion, is the real driver of adoption.

## 4. Evidence

_The signals (data, quotes, trends) that back the bet._

- Support tickets show users already working around the product's limits (separate accounts, manual payment splitting, side-channel availability syncing) — revealed behavior, not stated preference, and the strongest signal of real unmet demand.
- Drop-off is concentrated specifically in group booking sessions, showing intent to book exists but a structural gap (payments, coordination) is killing conversion at a specific step.
- Airbnb's own research found that roughly 4 out of 5 American travelers (79%) have been on multiple group trips in the last five years — group travel is a large, well-established behavior, not a niche one.
- Industry commentary describes Airbnb's Experiences relaunch as "whiplash" — paused, relaunched, paused, relaunched again across 2024–2025 — a documented, ongoing trust and consistency gap in the exact layer (curated group activities) Roamly is strongest in.
- Roamly's existing 3x industry-average repeat booking rate is a proprietary trust asset that no competitor in this set (Airbnb Experiences, GetYourGuide, WeTravel, or DIY) can currently claim with the same consistency.
