# Roamly Groups · GTM Strategy, Module 1 (Exercise 1)

## Responses
- **What is the core business problem Roamly is trying to solve with Roamly Groups?:** Coordinating group bookings across separate accounts, managing split payments manually and syncing availability
- **What does success look like if this launch goes well? Think beyond revenue, the product, the brand, and the customer relationship.:** Success looks like:
- Groups coordinate their entire trip inside Roamly instead of using outside tools
- Hosts benefit from higher-value, better-utilized bookings
- Group trips create organic growth by exposing new people to Roamly through friends
- Support tickets tied to manual group coordination drop
- Roamly's brand shifts from "app for solo travelers" to "platform for travel together"
- **What signals suggest this is a real market opportunity worth pursuing? What is the behavioral data telling you about unmet demand?:** Signals of real opportunity:
- Users are already working around the product's limits (separate accounts, manual splitting, side-channel syncing) — revealed behavior, not just a stated wish
- Drop-off is concentrated specifically in group sessions, showing intent exists but a structural gap is killing conversion
- 3x repeat booking rate proves trust already exists with individuals — the question is extending it to groups
- 12,000+ hosts across four continents gives enough supply density to support group logistics at scalex
- **List your top 5 possible segments that could benefit from Roamly Groups.:** - Friend groups / reunion travelers
- Bachelor/bachelorette & celebration trip groups
- Multi-generational family travel
- Corporate offsites / small team retreats
- Affinity/interest-based groups (alumni, clubs, online communities)
- **How might you size the market? Think industry reports, travel research, social listening, competitor analysis.:** Industry reports (Skift, Phocuswright) for the share of leisure travel that's group-coordinated vs. solo
Social listening in the same travel communities Roamly already sources users from, to gauge frequency and type of group-planning pain points
Competitor analysis — how Airbnb, TripIt, Splitwise handle group coordination, and what pricing/adoption they've validated
Internal data — mine existing bookings for proxy group behavior (same host/date bookings from multiple accounts, group-shaped support tickets by segment)
- **Which cross-functional stakeholders would you include to validate and execute? Think beyond product and engineering, who has context on the customer, market, and commercial model?:** Support/CX — owns the ticket data that surfaced the problem
Host Success/Supply Ops — can hosts absorb group booking demand
Payments/Finance & Trust & Safety — split-payment complexity, fraud, reconciliation
Data/Analytics — validate market sizing, instrument the launch
Marketing/Growth — knows which segment shows up in existing channels, referral potential
Sales/Partnerships — needed if pursuing a corporate segment
Legal/Compliance — liability and refund terms for multi-party payments
- **Which one segment is the most valuable to target first, and why?:** Friend groups / reunion travelers, with bachelor/bachelorette-style celebration trips as the entry wedge.
Why: closest to Roamly's existing user base, has a natural single-organizer champion already chasing payments and coordination, carries built-in urgency and shareability, and needs the simplest payment-splitting logic (even peer splits, not subsidized) — lowest risk for a first version.
- **What is the core value proposition for that segment?:** "Stop being the group's unpaid travel agent." Roamly Groups replaces chased Venmo requests and separate bookings with one shared link, automatic payment splitting, and a real-time shared itinerary — so the organizer plans less and the group still gets Roamly's curated, vetted-host experience.
