# Roamly Groups · Messaging Pillars & AI-Generated Asset, Module 4 (Exercise 2)

## Responses
- **Surface 1, copy variation:** In-app notification (in-product, moment of use)

"Booking a group activity? Split it with your friends — one link, everyone pays their own way, no accounts needed for them."

Leads with: the capability "one shared link + split payments without accounts." This surface catches someone mid-action — they've already found an experience and are about to book — so the notification's job isn't to sell the emotional relief, it's to remove a concrete point of friction right before they'd otherwise hit it (fronting the cost, or wondering if their friends need to sign up). Evidence and value prop would just be noise here; the person needs a functional nudge, not a pitch.
- **Surface 2, copy variation:** Social post (shareable, context-light)

"Me, organizing the group trip again: booking everyone's spot, chasing five different Venmo payments, texting the itinerary for the fourth time. Roamly Groups: one link, everyone pays their own way, one plan nobody has to ask about twice."

Leads with: the pain implied underneath the value prop, dramatized as a relatable moment, then resolved with the capabilities (one link, own payments, one plan) — not the abstract promise. Social has zero context and a three-second window, so it has to open with something instantly recognizable (the "me, organizing again" bit) before it can land the offer. The evidence never appears at all; nobody scrolling past a post is going to be persuaded by a repeat-booking statistic, and cluttering the post with it would kill the format the audience actually goes to social for.
- **Surface 3, copy variation:** Onboarding tooltip (first-run reinforcement)

"You've got the whole group booked and paid — nothing left to chase. This is what planning together should always feel like."

Leads with: the value prop, but reframed as a confirmation rather than a pitch. A tooltip fires right after someone completes the flow for the first time — they've just experienced the capabilities (the link, the split payments, the shared itinerary) firsthand, so there's no need to re-explain them. Its only job is to name what just happened and connect it to the emotional payoff, so the feeling ("I didn't have to chase anyone") gets attached to the product in that exact moment, while it's fresh. Leading with a capability here would be redundant — they just used it — and evidence has no place in a first-run moment; nobody wants a stat dropped on them right after finishing a task.
