# Roamly Groups · Pricing & Packaging Recommendation, Module 5 (Exercise 1)

## Responses
- **Pricing goal (Where to win first):** Staking out a defended position against the status quo and Airbnb — not entering a new market (the audience already exists and already travels together), and not maximizing revenue from a known audience (they're not being asked to spend more, just to not drop off or leak to a free alternative).
- **Why (Justification):** The M2 bet says Roamly Groups wins by becoming the system of record for group trips — a positioning claim, not a revenue claim. The battlecard showed the real competitors are free (Airbnb's split payments, the DIY status quo). Chasing revenue with a visible fee right now hands both an easy objection at the exact moment we're trying to win a switch. Better to remove price from the comparison, win on what can't be easily copied, and let revenue follow as larger per-trip transactions — not lead with it before the position is secured.
- **Pricing model (The model):** Transaction-based — value is experienced per booking event, not continuously. Organizers run a handful of group trips a year at most, so subscription doesn't fit; group size varies by occasion, so seat-based doesn't fit either. This also matches the M1 PLG motion: self-serve, minutes-to-value, no plan to pick before booking.
- **Who pays (Buyer vs. user):** Each traveler pays for their own experience booking — same as today, via Roamly's existing commission. The organizer (buyer/decision-maker) pays nothing extra for coordination. Buyer and end user experience value differently: the organizer's value is coordination relief (free), while each participant's value is the actual experience booked (paid, as always).
- **Packaging (The structure):** One audience from M4 (the trip organizer), so one structure — no tiers. The organizer needs to see that coordination is free and included, not a plan to evaluate. Tiering would force a decision the DIY stack never requires, undermining the "stop being the project manager" value prop before the trip is even booked.
- **Tiers (What each is for):** No Good-Better-Best ladder. Roamly Groups is a single, included capability layered onto the existing booking flow — not a separate product with tiers to route people toward. Every organizer gets the same thing: shared link, split payments, shared itinerary, no account required for guests. (If a second audience like corporate offsites is pursued later, it would warrant its own separate structure — not a tier of this one.)
- **Which research method (Van Westendorp, Max-Diff, Conjoint) would you commission, and what specific assumption are you trying to validate?:** Research method and assumption:

None of the three (Van Westendorp, Max-Diff, Conjoint) — they're all built to find a price point or feature value, and I've deliberately decided not to charge a visible fee. Instead, I'd commission a live usage pilot: launch to a slice of existing repeat bookers at zero incremental fee, and measure real behavior. The assumption being tested: that removing the fee is actually enough to convert organizers off Airbnb/DIY, at a transaction size that justifies the investment — not a pricing question, a conversion question.
- **What is the one thing in your recommendation that would change everything if you got it wrong?:** What would change everything if wrong:

That the "larger transaction" thesis holds — that a group trip actually generates meaningfully more revenue per trip (via commission across more participants) than an equivalent solo booking. If group trips don't convert at higher average revenue than solo ones, the entire model collapses: there's no monetization mechanism left, since we're explicitly not charging the organizer anything.
